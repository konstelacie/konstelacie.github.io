# Facebook Ad Entry Point

## Purpose of this document

This document defines the UX/UI and messaging role of the Facebook ad in the citimtedasom.sk funnel. It is intended to unify funnel thinking before implementation and to serve as a reference for ad copy, creative direction, and funnel coherence.

---

## Funnel context

- **One** Facebook ad
- **One** dedicated landing page
- **One** dedicated video on that landing page
- **Primary action:** Reserve a session and pay 10 €
- **Secondary actions:** Not part of v1. Email capture, chatbot, etc. are considered later, mainly for remarketing—not for the first-visit landing page UX.

---

## Role of the Facebook ad

The Facebook ad is the **entry point** to the funnel. Its job is to:

1. Open a specific problem or emotional struggle the target audience recognises
2. Promise that an explanation or new perspective exists
3. Direct the user to the landing page video—not to a complex decision or sale

The ad does **not** sell the session. It sells the **next step**: watching the video.

---

## What the ad should do

- **Hook** with a relatable problem, pattern, or emotional struggle
- **Reframe** or hint at a different perspective
- **Point** clearly to the video as the place where the explanation lives
- **Use a simple CTA** such as “Watch video” / “Pozri video” (or equivalent)
- Stay **short and scannable** for feed context
- Align tone and language with the landing page and video

---

## What the ad should NOT do

- Fully sell the session or explain the offer in detail
- Promise outcomes that the video or landing page do not deliver
- Direct users straight to a reservation form or payment
- Introduce secondary actions (email capture, chatbot, etc.)
- Overload with multiple CTAs or competing messages
- Use vague or generic hooks that don’t match the video content

---

## Recommended message structure

1. **Problem / pattern / struggle** — One clear, specific thing the audience recognises
2. **Perspective / reframe** — A brief hint that there’s another way to see it
3. **Bridge to video** — “The full explanation is in the video” (or equivalent)
4. **CTA** — Watch the video

Keep copy tight. The ad is a bridge, not a brochure.

---

## Relationship between ad and landing page

- The ad and landing page must feel like **one continuous experience**
- Messaging, tone, and problem framing should be consistent
- The landing page should immediately reinforce what the ad promised: “Here’s the video”
- No jarring shift in language, visuals, or value proposition

---

## Relationship between ad and landing page video

- The ad **promises**; the video **delivers**
- The video should:
  1. Give real value first (explanation, perspective, reframing)
  2. Then transition to the session invitation
- The ad should not repeat video content; it should create curiosity and expectation
- If the ad says “the explanation is in the video,” the video must provide that explanation before asking for action

---

## Primary CTA logic

- **Ad CTA:** Watch the video
- **Landing page primary CTA:** Reserve a session and pay 10 €
- The ad does not ask for reservation or payment. That happens after the video has delivered value and introduced the session offer.

---

## Why the ad should lead to the video, not directly to a complex decision

- **Cognitive load:** A cold audience is not ready to decide on a paid session from an ad alone
- **Trust:** The video builds trust by giving value first
- **Clarity:** The video explains the perspective and the offer in a structured way
- **Conversion quality:** Users who watch and then reserve are more committed than those who click and pay immediately
- **Funnel logic:** Ad → video → landing page CTA creates a clear, single path with one primary action per step

---

## Example ad patterns

| Pattern | Structure | Example direction |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Problem → perspective → watch video** | State a problem; hint at a different angle; invite to watch | “You keep hitting the same wall. There’s a reason—and a way to see it differently. Watch the video.” |
| **Repeated life pattern → explanation → watch video** | Describe a recurring pattern; promise an explanation | “The same situation keeps coming back. Here’s what’s actually going on—and what you can do. See the full explanation in the video.” |
| **Emotional struggle → reframing → watch video** | Name an emotional struggle; offer a reframe | “You feel stuck even when nothing ‘big’ is wrong. A simple shift in perspective can change that. Watch the video to see how.” |

These are patterns, not final copy. Adapt to Slovak, audience, and brand voice.
