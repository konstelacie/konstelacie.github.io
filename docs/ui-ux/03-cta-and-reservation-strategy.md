# CTA and Reservation Strategy

## Purpose of this document

This document defines how the primary call-to-action works and how users transition from watching the video to reserving a session in the citimtedasom.sk funnel. It covers CTA philosophy, placement, wording, and the handoff to the reservation system.

---

## CTA philosophy

The funnel is designed around a **single primary action** to reduce decision friction.

- One clear next step at every stage: ad → watch video; landing page → reserve session
- No competing buttons, no "or do this instead"
- Users who are ready can act immediately; users who need more info find it on the same page
- The goal is to make "reserve a session" feel like the obvious, natural next step after the video

---

## Primary CTA definition

**"Reserve a session – 10 €"** (or Slovak equivalent: e.g. "Rezervovať session – 10 €")

The CTA should:

- **Be simple** — One action, one outcome. No ambiguity.
- **Reference the small commitment (10 €)** — The price is part of the CTA so users know the cost before clicking. No surprise at checkout.
- **Feel safe and low-pressure** — "Reserve" implies a small step, not a big purchase. The 10 € amount reinforces that this is a low-risk commitment.

---

## CTA placement on the landing page

Recommended placement:

1. **Immediately below the video** — Capitalise on post-video intent. The user has just heard the invitation; the button is right there.
2. **After the "How the session works" section** — For users who scroll to understand the format first.
3. **After the pricing explanation** — For users who want clarity on cost before committing.
4. **At the very bottom of the page** — For users who read everything (FAQ, etc.) and are ready to act.

**Why repeating the same CTA reduces friction during scrolling:**

- Users don't have to scroll back up to find the button
- Each section can end with a natural "next step" — the same CTA
- Different users convert at different scroll depths; multiple placements increase the chance they see the CTA when they're ready

---

## CTA wording guidelines

**Good wording examples:**

- Reserve a session – 10 €
- Reserve your session
- Choose a time

**Why wording should stay consistent across the page:**

- One phrase = one mental model. "Reserve a session" means the same thing everywhere
- Variation ("Book now" vs. "Reserve" vs. "Get started") creates confusion about what happens next
- Consistency reinforces the single-action funnel

Use the same primary wording for all CTA instances. Secondary phrasing (e.g. "Choose a time") can support the main CTA in context but should not replace it as the primary label.

---

## Transition from CTA to reservation system

**Flow:** Landing page → reservation page → calendar / time selection → payment

After clicking the CTA:

1. User lands on the reservation interface (same page or dedicated page)
2. Sees available slots (calendar or list)
3. Selects a time
4. Proceeds to payment (10 €)
5. Receives confirmation

**Why the reservation interface should:**

- **Be simple** — Minimal fields. No long forms before seeing slots.
- **Show available slots quickly** — Users should see options within one or two clicks. Delays increase drop-off.
- **Minimise extra steps** — No unnecessary account creation, lengthy questionnaires, or multi-step flows before payment. Reserve → pay → done.

## Psychological barriers before clicking CTA

Common user doubts:

- "What will happen in the session?"
- "Is this for me?"
- "What if it doesn't help?"
- "Is 10 € the full price or just a deposit?"
- "What if I pick the wrong time?"

**How the landing page addresses these:**

- **"How the session works"** — Answers "What will happen?" Format, duration, what to expect.
- **"Who this is for / not for"** — Answers "Is this for me?" Qualifies the audience.
- **FAQ** — Answers "What if it doesn't help?", pricing questions, cancellation, etc.
- **Pricing explanation** — Clarifies that 10 € is the reservation payment and what the full recommended price is.

These sections are placed *before* or *around* the CTA so users can resolve doubts without leaving the page. The CTA appears when they're ready.

---

## Price communication logic

**Model:**

1. User reserves with 10 €
2. Session happens
3. Full recommended price is explained on the page (before reservation)

**Why the small reservation payment lowers the barrier:**

- 10 € feels like a low-risk trial, not a full commitment
- Users can "try" the offer without a large upfront investment
- The page should clearly state what 10 € covers (reservation, session) and what the full price is, so there are no surprises

---

## What we intentionally avoid in v1

- **Alternative CTAs** — No "Contact us," "Learn more," or "Sign up for newsletter" as primary or competing actions
- **Email signup** — No lead capture before or instead of reservation
- **Chatbot** — No chat widget that could divert attention
- **Multiple booking options** — One session type, one price point. No "Choose your package" or tier selection in v1

**Reason:** Keep the funnel focused on a single decision. Every extra option increases cognitive load and dilutes the primary action. Secondary paths can be added later based on data and remarketing needs.

## Future experiments

Possible UX experiments for later iterations:

- **"Not sure yet" option** — A subtle link or secondary path for users who want to stay in touch without reserving (e.g. email capture). Must not compete visually with the primary CTA.
- **Testing different CTA wording** — e.g. "Reserve a session – 10 €" vs. "Choose your time – 10 €" vs. "Book a session"
- **Testing placement of the first CTA** — e.g. CTA beside the video vs. only below; sticky CTA on scroll vs. static placement
- **Testing CTA frequency** — How many repetitions optimise conversion without feeling pushy
