# User Doubts and FAQ

## Purpose of this document

This document defines how the landing page addresses the most common doubts users have before reserving a session. Many users do not reserve immediately because of **uncertainty rather than lack of interest**. The goal is to reduce hesitation through clear, honest content placed at the right points in the funnel.

---

## Typical user doubts

Common doubts before clicking the CTA:

- **What exactly will happen during the session?** — Users need a concrete picture. Vague descriptions increase anxiety.
- **Is this really for me?** — Users wonder if the offer fits their situation. Without clarity, they may self-disqualify.
- **Will I feel pressured or uncomfortable?** — Fear of being sold to or judged. The page should signal safety and low pressure.
- **What if it doesn't help?** — Fear of wasting time or money. Users need to know what happens if the session doesn't meet their expectations.

**Why these doubts must be addressed clearly on the landing page:**

- Users who leave without reserving often do so because of unanswered questions, not because they're uninterested
- The landing page is the only place to address these before users bounce
- Clear answers reduce friction and increase the likelihood of conversion when the user is ready

---

## How the landing page reduces uncertainty

Different sections answer different doubts:

| Section | Addresses |
|---------|-----------|
| **How the session works** | What happens during the session, format, duration, what to expect |
| **Who this is for / not for** | Is this for me? Qualifies the audience and sets expectations |
| **Pricing explanation** | What 10 € covers, full price, no hidden costs |
| **FAQ** | Edge cases, objections, "what if" scenarios that don't fit the main sections |

These sections should appear **before the final CTA**. Users who scroll down are often seeking reassurance. The FAQ and supporting content should be in reach before they reach the bottom CTA.

---

## FAQ role in conversion

The FAQ should not be a random list of questions. It should address the **most likely reasons someone would hesitate** before reserving.

- Prioritise questions that block conversion
- Avoid generic or rarely asked questions
- Each FAQ entry should answer a real doubt that could prevent a click

---

## Example FAQ questions

- **How does the session work?** — Short description of format, what happens step by step.
- **How long does the session last?** — Concrete duration.
- **What if I'm not sure what my problem is?** — Reassurance that the session can help clarify; no need to have everything figured out.
- **What happens after the session?** — What to expect next, follow-up, no pressure.
- **What if I decide the session wasn't helpful?** — Honest answer about refunds, expectations, or next steps. Transparency builds trust.

**How short, honest answers increase trust:**

- Long, defensive answers feel like a sales pitch
- Short, direct answers signal confidence and honesty
- "I don't know" or "It depends" where appropriate is better than overpromising

---

## Tone and style of answers

FAQ answers should:

- **Be calm and clear** — No urgency, no pressure. Inform, don't persuade.
- **Avoid marketing language** — No "amazing," "transformative," or superlatives. Stick to facts.
- **Avoid exaggeration** — Don't promise outcomes the session doesn't guarantee.
- **Focus on transparency and safety** — Acknowledge limitations. "This is not for everyone" is more credible than "This works for everyone."

---

## Placement of FAQ on the page

**Recommended placement:** After pricing explanation and before the final CTA.

**Why this placement:**

- Users who have read "How it works," "Who it's for," and pricing are still on the page — they're interested but hesitant
- FAQ catches their remaining objections before they reach the bottom CTA
- The final CTA then appears after they've had a chance to resolve doubts

---

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Overly long explanations** — Each answer should be concise. One or two short paragraphs max.
- **Abstract descriptions instead of concrete steps** — "We explore your situation" is vague. "We talk through your situation, ask clarifying questions, and work toward a clearer perspective" is concrete.
- **Trying to answer too many questions** — A long FAQ dilutes focus. Start with 5–7 high-impact questions. Add more only if real user questions justify it.

---

## Possible future improvements

- **Collecting real user questions over time** — From support, chat, or post-session feedback. Use these to refine the FAQ.
- **Refining FAQ based on support messages** — If the same question appears repeatedly, add it to the FAQ.
- **Adding real client concerns anonymously** — "Others often ask: …" can make the FAQ feel more grounded and relatable.
