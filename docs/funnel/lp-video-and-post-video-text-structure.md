# LP Flow: Video Part 2 + Post-Video Text

**Status:** Current direction — not final locked implementation.

This note captures how we are thinking about the landing-page sequence after a Facebook ad: what each block is for, how roles split between video and text, and where decisions remain open. It is internal product, funnel, and messaging guidance—not finished copy for the page or scripts.

---

## 1. Overall flow

The intended sequence is:

1. **Facebook ad** — Problem hook and enough curiosity to click through.
2. **LP video part 1** — Delivers the promised value: insight, orientation, or a small piece of help aligned with what the ad promised.
3. **LP video part 2** — Moves from that free value toward an invitation to work together, without feeling like a separate “sales segment.”
4. **Text below the video** — Continues the same story for people who need more clarity, detail, or reassurance before they act.

The goal is **natural continuation**: one line of help from ad → first video → second video → text. We are not aiming for a sharp cut from “content” to “pitch.” The transition should feel like the same conversation deepening, not a hard switch into selling.

---

## 2. Role of LP video part 2

LP video part 2 is **not** meant to work like a traditional standalone sales pitch. Its job is to act as a **bridge** from the free insight (part 1) into a **soft invitation** to a paid session.

**Direction we want to preserve:**

- It should feel like a **continuation of help**, not a separate block whose only job is to close.
- It should **acknowledge limits** of short-form video: what can and cannot be done in a few minutes.
- It should **explain what changes** when someone works directly with Michal—what the session is for in practice, without turning into a brochure.
- It should help the viewer **recognize fit**: signals that this is for them, and honest signals that it may not be.
- It should **end with a low-pressure invitation**: clear enough to act on, without pressure or urgency tricks.

**Suggested internal structure** (for planning scripts and edits—not a fixed template):

1. Bridge / transition from the free insight (same voice, same thread).
2. Limitation of short-form help (why deeper work needs another format).
3. How the session works / what Michal actually does with people (concrete, human, not abstract).
4. Who it is for / who it is not for (fit and boundaries).
5. Soft CTA (what to do next, without hard sell).
6. Pressure reduction / transparent payment framing (so “next step” does not feel like a trap).

Exact wording, length, and order may still shift; the point is to keep these functions in mind so part 2 stays a bridge, not a pitch deck on camera.

---

## 3. Role of the text below the video

The text below the video should **not** repeat the video beat-for-beat. It complements the video: same intent, different job.

**It mainly supports people who:**

- Skipped, skimmed, or only partially watched the video.
- Are interested but uncertain whether to book or what they are signing up for.
- Need **more rational, concrete clarification** before clicking (process, practicalities, “what happens if…”).

**Key ideas to hold:**

- The text is a **continuation**, not duplication: it picks up themes without re-reading the script.
- It should **validate hesitation and uncertainty** as reasonable, not as failure to “get it.”
- It should briefly address **why the issue may not have shifted yet** after the video alone (without blaming the viewer).
- It should clarify **what is different** about this approach compared to generic self-help or advice.
- It should explain **concretely how a session works** (flow, expectations at a high level).
- It should give **transparent practical details** (booking, payment, what they need to know to decide).
- It should **support the final action** (e.g. reservation or next step) **without pressure**.

**Suggested internal structure** (planning aid):

1. Gentle opening (tone match with the videos; no abrupt marketing headline required in principle—see open questions).
2. Validation of hesitation (“it’s normal to wonder…”).
3. Why this may have been hard to solve alone (context, not pathology).
4. What is different in this approach (distinctive, not hype).
5. Practical details of the session (what actually happens).
6. Payment / reservation clarity (transparent, plain language).
7. Soft CTA (clear next step, low pressure).

---

## 4. Core messaging principle

**Principle:** This funnel is **not** designed as a classic aggressive sales funnel.

The intended feel:

**problem → mini experience / insight → trust → invitation**

The free part (ad promise + LP video part 1) should land as **real value**, not as a teaser whose only purpose is to unlock a pitch. LP video part 2 and the text should read as **the same helpful stance**, narrowing toward “if this fits you, here’s how we can go deeper”—not “now the real selling starts.”

Users should not be left thinking:

- “The free part was only bait.”
- “Everything up to here was setup; now I’m being sold to in a different mode.”

We may still invite clearly; the distinction is **continuity of stance and generosity**, not hiding that a paid session exists.

---

## 5. Practical UX implication

**Recommended split of roles:**

- **Video (parts 1 and 2):** Builds **trust** and **emotional continuity**—voice, presence, pacing, and a sense of being met as a person.
- **Text below the video:** Supports **decision clarity**—structure, detail, scanning, and answers to “what exactly am I agreeing to?”

The **end of the video** can naturally **point downward** to the text for practical details (“If you want the specifics—how it works, how booking and payment work—that’s spelled out just below.”). That keeps the video from carrying every practical bullet while still connecting the two layers.

---

## 6. Open questions / not yet locked

These are intentionally unresolved; they should be decided with testing, stakeholder input, and alignment with reservation and pricing docs.

- **Explicitness of the invitation in video part 2:** How direct should the ask be vs. implied? Where is the line between “clear” and “pushy” for this audience?
- **Practical detail in video vs. text:** How much booking, pricing, and process detail belongs in part 2 vs. reserved for the text only?
- **Text opening:** Should the block start **without a headline**, or with a **very soft** headline— and how does that interact with SEO and scanning behavior?
- **Emphasis of pricing and reservation** across layers: Relative weight of price, deposit, cancellation, and booking steps in part 2 vs. post-video text vs. later steps in the funnel.

---

## Style alignment (for authors)

Use neutral, thoughtful wording. Keep alignment with a **trust-based, low-pressure** path. This document is **not** ad copy, script, or implementation spec.
