# 018 — Life Autopilot Assessment — Experience Specification (v1)

## Purpose

This document defines the **experience** of completing the Life Autopilot Assessment.

It does **not** describe implementation details.

It describes what the participant should **think**, **feel**, and **experience** from the first click until the personalized result appears.

The assessment should feel less like completing a questionnaire and more like moving through a guided diagnostic experience.

---

# Experience Philosophy

The user should never feel that they are:

* filling out a survey
* completing a psychological test
* answering random questions

Instead, they should feel that they are gradually discovering something meaningful about themselves.

Every screen should reduce friction and increase curiosity.

---

# Emotional Journey

The assessment is designed to guide users through six emotional phases.

```
Curiosity

↓

Recognition

↓

Reflection

↓

Commitment

↓

Anticipation

↓

Clarity
```

Every interaction should support this progression.

---

# Phase 1 — Beginning

## Objective

Reduce uncertainty.

Increase trust.

Encourage the first click.

## User Thoughts

"I wonder what this will reveal."

"This feels different from a personality quiz."

## Experience

After clicking:

> Chcem zistiť svoj výsledok

the interface should become extremely minimal.

No marketing.

No navigation.

No unnecessary elements.

Only:

* progress
* question
* answers

The assessment has begun.

---

# Phase 2 — Early Questions (1–4)

## Objective

Create rhythm.

The user should stop evaluating the assessment and simply answer.

Questions should feel:

* relatable
* easy
* non-threatening

Avoid making users think too hard.

The experience should become almost automatic.

---

## Progress

Display:

Question 1 of 24

Progress bar

Estimated time remaining

Example:

≈ 3 minutes remaining

This reduces uncertainty.

---

## Guidance

A small note below the progress bar:

> There are no right or wrong answers. Choose the option that best reflects your everyday experience.

This reduces performance anxiety.

---

# Phase 3 — First Reflection

After Question 4

The assessment pauses.

Not with a popup.

Not with a notification.

With a dedicated reflection screen.

---

Example:

## A moment to reflect

Life rarely switches to autopilot overnight.

More often, it happens through hundreds of small routines that slowly become invisible.

Button:

Continue

---

Purpose:

The participant stops answering mechanically.

They start observing themselves.

---

# Phase 4 — Deep Engagement

Questions 5–20

The participant is now invested.

The interface should disappear.

Nothing should interrupt the flow except the planned reflection moments.

Interaction should become effortless.

Click.

Next question.

Click.

Next question.

Animation:

150–250 ms

No loading screens.

No "Next" button.

The answer itself advances the assessment.

---

# Reflection Moments

Reflection screens appear after:

Question 4

Question 8

Question 12

Question 16

Question 20

Purpose:

* reduce fatigue
* reward progress
* increase perceived value
* reinforce methodology

Each reflection should feel like an observation—not advice.

---

# Phase 5 — Final Questions

Questions 21–24

Psychology changes.

The participant now wants the result.

Increase anticipation.

Progress bar should clearly communicate that the end is close.

Example:

Question 23 of 24

Almost there.

---

Avoid congratulating too early.

Simply communicate progress.

---

# Phase 6 — Analysis

Immediately after Question 24

Do **not** show the email form immediately.

Instead create a short transition.

---

Example

## Analyzing your responses...

We are identifying the patterns that most strongly influence your current life system.

Animated progress indicator.

Duration:

1.5–2.5 seconds.

This is not fake loading.

It creates the feeling that something meaningful is happening.

---

# Email Unlock

Only after the analysis screen.

Headline:

## Your personalized assessment is ready.

Short explanation:

Enter your email to unlock your results and receive your personalized Life Autopilot assessment.

Emphasize value.

Not obligation.

---

"What did you discover?"

Not:

"What should you buy?"

Structure:

Primary Bottleneck

↓

Visual Life System

↓

Meaning

↓

Blind Spot

↓

Long-term trajectory

↓

First Small Step

↓

Life Autopilot Diagnosis

---

# Visual Principles

During the assessment:

Hide:

* footer
* navigation
* legal links
* distractions

Show only:

* progress
* question
* answers
* back button

Everything else competes with completion.

---

# Motion Design

Animation should be subtle.

Suggested transitions:

Question slides upward.

Next question fades in.

Duration:

150–250 ms.

Avoid:

* dramatic effects
* bouncing
* excessive movement

Motion should support flow.

Not attract attention.

---

# Question Presentation

The question is the most important element.

It should be visually dominant.

Answers should feel easy to scan.

Avoid unnecessary numbering.

Preferred style:

○ Strongly disagree

○ Disagree

○ Neither agree nor disagree

○ Agree

○ Strongly agree

The interface should disappear behind the content.

---

# Progress Psychology

Never allow the participant to wonder:

How many questions remain?

Always communicate progress.

Progress creates motivation.

Uncertainty creates abandonment.

---

# Reflection Psychology

Reflection screens are critical.

They transform the experience from:

"I am answering questions."

to

"I am beginning to understand something."

This shift dramatically increases perceived value.

---

# Anticipation Psychology

The final questions should create excitement.

Not pressure.

The participant should naturally think:

"I'm curious what this will show."

Not:

"I just want this finished."

---

# Result Psychology

The first seconds after opening the results matter most.

The participant should experience:

Recognition.

Not surprise.

Not confusion.

Not sales pressure.

Recognition builds trust.

Trust opens the door to the paid diagnosis.

---

# The Golden Rule

Every screen should answer one question:

"What does the participant need to feel right now?"

Not:

"What information do we want to communicate?"

Experience always comes before explanation.

---

# Success Criteria

When users finish the assessment, they should feel able to say:

* That was easier than I expected.
* Those questions really made me think.
* This felt different from a normal online test.
* The result describes me surprisingly well.
* I want to understand why this pattern exists.

If these five reactions occur consistently, the assessment has achieved its purpose.

---

# Closing Principle

The assessment is not a questionnaire.

It is the first experience of the Life Autopilot Methodology.

The participant should leave with more than a score.

They should leave with the feeling that they have just seen something about themselves that had been quietly present all along—but until now, remained invisible.
