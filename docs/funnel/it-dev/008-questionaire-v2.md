# Life Autopilot Diagnosis — Questionnaire & Implementation Strategy (v2)

## Vision

The Life Autopilot Assessment is **not a quiz**, **not a personality test**, and **not a lead form**.

It is the first experience of the **Life Autopilot Methodology**.

Its purpose is to move people from:

> *"Something feels wrong."*

to

> *"Now I finally understand what's happening."*

The assessment should create an immediate sense of clarity and recognition that naturally leads into the paid **Life Autopilot Diagnosis**, where the deeper causes are explored.

---

# Core Positioning

We are **not diagnosing people.**

We are diagnosing the **current state of their life system.**

This distinction is fundamental.

Instead of saying:

> "You are a High Performer."

we say:

> "Your current life system is primarily affected by an Identity Loop."

The focus is on **patterns**, not personality.

Patterns can change.

Systems can be improved.

People are not broken.

---

The assessment should never feel like filling out a form.

# Step 1 — Meta Ads

The advertisements should create curiosity, not sell coaching.

The objective is to make people think:

> "That sounds exactly like me."

Examples:

* Why does life feel full but not fulfilling?
* You're productive all day—but why do you still feel stuck?
* What if the problem isn't motivation, but the way your life system currently operates?

The ad should invite people to discover their current bottleneck.

CTA:

> Take the Free Life Autopilot Assessment

# Step 3 — Interactive Assessment

## Experience Principles

The assessment should feel like software, not a survey.

Design principles:

* one question per screen
* clean interface
* progress bar
* smooth transitions
* mobile-first
* no distractions

Twenty-four questions should never feel overwhelming.

Instead people simply answer:

> "One more question."

---

# Assessment Structure

24 questions

6 questions for each dimension:

* Autopilot
* Identity
* Energy
* Relationships

All questions use the same five-point Likert scale.

* Strongly disagree
* Disagree
* Neutral
* Agree
* Strongly agree

---

# Question Principles

Every question should describe an observable experience.

Avoid abstract psychology.

Instead of:

> I don't know who I am anymore.

Use:

> When someone asks what I want, I usually think about everyone else's needs first.

Questions should measure patterns rather than temporary emotions.

---

# Micro-Insights

Every four to five questions, display a short insight before continuing.

Purpose:

* reduce abandonment
* maintain curiosity
* create small "aha" moments
* increase perceived value
* demonstrate expertise without lecturing

Example:

> Many people don't consciously ignore themselves. They simply become so skilled at solving everyone else's problems that their own needs gradually disappear from view.

These insights transform the experience from "answering questions" into "learning about yourself."

---

# Email Capture Strategy

The assessment should **not** request an email before the questions begin.

Instead:

Landing Page

↓

Assessment

↓

"We've finished analyzing your responses..."

↓

Enter your email to unlock your personalized diagnosis.

This works because users have already invested several minutes and now want to see the outcome.

The email is exchanged for value—not demanded upfront.

---

# Scoring Philosophy

Do not calculate one total score.

Instead calculate four independent dimensions.

Autopilot

Identity

Energy

Relationships

Each dimension receives its own score.

The assessment identifies:

* Primary Bottleneck
* Secondary Bottleneck

This produces results that feel nuanced and realistic.

# Transition to the Paid Diagnosis

The assessment answers:

> **What appears to be happening?**

The paid diagnosis answers:

> **Why is it happening?**

This distinction is essential.

The free assessment identifies patterns.

The paid diagnosis explores:

* how the pattern developed
* unconscious drivers
* reinforcing habits
* interactions between all four systems
* highest-leverage interventions
* personalized roadmap

The paid session should feel like the natural next chapter—not a sales pitch.

---

* assessment

The four dimensions should become a recognizable visual model.

Imagine a circular "Life Autopilot System" showing the relationship between:

* Autopilot
* Identity
* Energy
* Relationships

Every assessment, report, workshop, presentation, and coaching session should use the same visual framework.

Rather than embedding a generic form, build a dedicated interactive assessment.

Recommended capabilities:

* one question per screen
* progress bar
* animated transitions
* responsive mobile design
* micro-insights between question blocks
* automatic scoring
* dynamic personalized results
* email integration
* CRM integration
* calendar booking integration
* analytics dashboard
* completion tracking
* drop-off analysis
* conversion tracking
* bottleneck distribution reporting

The assessment should function as a digital product—not a form.

---

# Analytics & Continuous Improvement

The assessment should improve continuously through real-world data.

Track metrics such as:

* Landing page conversion rate
* Assessment start rate
* Completion rate
* Email unlock rate
* Paid diagnosis booking rate
* Most common primary bottlenecks
* Most common bottleneck combinations
* Question-level abandonment
* Time spent per question

Insights from analytics should guide future iterations.

---

# Versioning Philosophy

Version 1 is the foundation.

After the first 20–30 paid diagnoses:

* compare assessment results with real conversations
* refine wording
* validate scoring
* identify recurring combinations
* improve recommendations
* adjust thresholds
* strengthen the methodology

Over time, the assessment evolves from a lead-generation asset into a validated proprietary diagnostic framework.
