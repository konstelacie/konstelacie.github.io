# 023 – Email Architecture (Post-Assessment Nurture)

**Status:** Strategy / CRM Architecture  
**Purpose:** Define the post-assessment email journey from completed Assessment to Paid Diagnosis.  
**Depends on:** 022 Messaging Architecture, 020 Customer Journey, 018 Assessment Psychology, 015 Principles, 010 Decisions, EMAILING.md  
**Does not contain:** Final email copy.

---

# Purpose

This document translates the Messaging Architecture into a structured email journey.

Unlike **022**, which defines *what must change*, this document defines **how the email sequence facilitates that change over time**.

It answers questions such as:

- How many emails?
- When are they sent?
- What is the purpose of each email?
- What psychological shift should occur?
- When is the paid diagnosis introduced?
- Which parts are universal and which are personalized?

---

# Guiding Principle

The objective of the sequence is **not conversion**.

The objective is to help participants naturally reach the conclusion:

> *"I understand what is happening.
Now I want to understand why."*

If this happens, conversion becomes a consequence rather than the goal.

---

# Sequence Overview

The nurture sequence consists of **seven emails**.

Each email has exactly one job.

No email attempts to achieve multiple psychological shifts.

| Email | Primary Goal |
|--------|--------------|
| E1 | Preserve recognition |
| E2 | Reframe the problem |
| E3 | Introduce the limits of the assessment |
| E4 | Create curiosity about the origin of patterns |
| E5 | Explain the role of the paid diagnosis |
| E6 | Reduce uncertainty and objections |
| E7 | Invite the participant to take the next step |

---

# Email Progression

The sequence follows a strict progression:

Recognition

↓

Reflection

↓

Reframing

↓

Curiosity

↓

Understanding

↓

Confidence

↓

Decision

No email should skip a stage.

---

# Email Framework

Every email should answer four questions:

1. What psychological state is the participant in?
2. What belief should change?
3. What new question should remain?
4. How strong should the CTA be?

---

# Email 1

## Purpose

Preserve the emotional state immediately after the assessment.

The participant should feel that the assessment was understood correctly and that the experience was valid.

---

### Participant State

"I've just seen my results."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> Maybe this is another personality test.

To:

> This actually describes something real.

---

### Open Question

> If this is true, why does it happen?

---

### CTA

None.

The email should not introduce the paid diagnosis.

---

### Personalization

High.

Include:

- primary bottleneck
- recognition paragraph
- one reflection

Everything else remains universal.

---

# Email 2

## Purpose

Separate patterns from personality.

Introduce the concept of adaptive life patterns.

---

### Participant State

"I recognize myself."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> That's just who I am.

To:

> These patterns probably developed over time.

---

### Open Question

> How do these patterns actually develop?

---

### CTA

None.

---

### Personalization

Medium.

Examples change by bottleneck.

Core message remains identical.

---

# Email 3

## Purpose

Introduce the intentional limitation of the assessment.

---

### Participant State

"I understand the pattern."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> I already know the answer.

To:

> I only know the visible part.

---

### Open Question

> What causes these patterns beneath the surface?

---

### CTA

Very soft.

Link to learn more about the paid diagnosis (future landing page).

No invitation to book.

---

### Personalization

Low.

This message is mostly universal.

---

# Email 4

## Purpose

Shift attention from symptoms to systems.

---

### Participant State

"I want deeper understanding."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> I should fix the symptom.

To:

> I should understand the system.

---

### Open Question

> Which part of my system creates the biggest impact?

---

### CTA

Soft.

Mention that this is exactly what the paid diagnosis explores.

---

### Personalization

Medium.

Use bottleneck-specific examples where relevant.

---

# Email 5

## Purpose

Position the paid diagnosis.

This is the first email where the paid product becomes the central topic.

---

### Participant State

"I think I need more than the assessment."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> It's another coaching session.

To:

> It's an investigation of my life system.

---

### Open Question

> What would I discover during the diagnosis?

---

### CTA

Soft invitation.

Request information / Join the waitlist.

---

### Personalization

Low.

The explanation of the product should remain consistent.

---

# Email 6

## Purpose

Reduce uncertainty.

Address the questions participants naturally have before taking action.

---

### Participant State

"I might do this."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> I'm not sure this is for me.

To:

> I understand exactly what it is.

---

### Typical Objections

- Is this therapy?
- Is this coaching?
- Will I get advice?
- What happens during the session?
- Who is it for?
- Who is it not for?

---

### CTA

Soft.

Waitlist / Request information.

---

### Personalization

Minimal.

---

# Email 7

## Purpose

Allow the participant to make a decision.

This email is not about creating urgency.

It is about providing clarity.

---

### Participant State

"I understand the assessment and the diagnosis."

---

### Belief Shift

From:

> Maybe someday.

To:

> I know what my next step is.

---

### CTA

Primary.

Request information.

Join the waitlist.

---

### Personalization

Minimal.

---

# CTA Escalation

| Email | CTA Intensity |
|---------|--------------|
| E1 | None |
| E2 | None |
| E3 | Very Soft |
| E4 | Soft |
| E5 | Medium |
| E6 | Medium |
| E7 | Primary |

The participant should never feel that a product is being sold.

Instead, each CTA should feel like the natural continuation of the previous insight.

# Psychological Progression

| Email | Emotional State |
|--------|-----------------|
| E1 | Recognition |
| E2 | Relief |
| E3 | Curiosity |
| E4 | Understanding |
| E5 | Confidence |
| E6 | Readiness |
| E7 | Decision |

---

# Belief Progression

| Email | Core Belief Shift |
|--------|-------------------|
| E1 | This feels accurate. |
| E2 | These are patterns, not personality. |
| E3 | The assessment has intentional limits. |
| E4 | Systems matter more than symptoms. |
| E5 | The diagnosis answers a different question. |
| E6 | I understand what the diagnosis is. |
| E7 | This is the logical next step. |

---

# Open Question Progression

Every email intentionally leaves one meaningful question unanswered.

| Email | Open Question |
|--------|---------------|
| E1 | Why does this happen? |
| E2 | Where do these patterns come from? |
| E3 | What can't the assessment reveal? |
| E4 | Which part of my system matters most? |
| E5 | What happens during the diagnosis? |
| E6 | Is this right for me? |
| E7 | Am I ready to understand more? |

These questions should emerge naturally rather than being explicitly asked.

---

# Universal vs. Personalized Content

## Universal (≈80%)

Shared across all participants:

- messaging
- philosophy
- systems thinking
- assessment limitations
- diagnosis explanation
- CTA
- structure

---

## Personalized (≈20%)

Dynamic elements:

- primary bottleneck
- optional secondary bottleneck
- examples
- metaphors
- reflection prompts
- recognition paragraphs

The overall narrative never changes.

---

# Timing Principles

Exact timing is intentionally defined outside this document and may evolve through testing.

The sequence should follow these principles:

- The first email arrives while recognition is still emotionally present.
- Early emails are closer together to preserve momentum.
- Later emails allow more time for reflection.
- CTA intensity increases only after curiosity has developed.
- The participant should never feel rushed.

---

# Exit Conditions

The sequence ends when one of the following occurs:

- Participant requests information.
- Participant joins the waitlist.
- Participant books the paid diagnosis (future).
- Participant unsubscribes from marketing communication.

Future CRM logic may branch participants into additional journeys.

---

# Success Criteria

The architecture succeeds when participants reach the end of the sequence and conclude:

> "The assessment helped me understand what is happening."

and

> "The paid diagnosis seems like the natural next step if I want to understand why."

If those conclusions are reached without sales pressure, the sequence has achieved its purpose.
